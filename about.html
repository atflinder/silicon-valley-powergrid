ANDREW FLINDERS · SOURCES & METHODS

What the data can show.

The study snapshot was preserved in November 2026. The website makes the existing evidence explorable; it does not query current electricity or weather feeds.

Keep the geographies separate

  • CAISO: hourly regional demand for 2021–2025, with San Jose model weather as a local indicator. The daily chart retains 1,813 complete Pacific-local dates.
  • Santa Clara County: monthly consumption for 2019–2024. Each map selection uses one countywide energy rate, divided by actual calendar days.
  • City of Santa Clara utility: retail sales for January 2019–August 2026. The 2026 data are partial and preliminary.
  • Current geography: boundaries, voltage ratings, zoning, and historic inventories provide context. They are not historical neighborhood electricity measurements.

Time, gaps, and comparison limits

UTC instants remain hourly join keys. Pacific local dates supply calendar groups. Spring skips an hour and fall repeats one, so a complete local day can have 23, 24, or 25 intervals. Missing demand is never replaced by zero. County winter groups January, February, and December of the same selected year.

The 2024 EIA API/workbook check has 17,467 matching nonmissing pairs, with different completeness. The selected direct CAISO operator comparison still has an unresolved timing/definition issue. These sources remain separate. The patterns do not estimate causal COVID-19 or AI-only energy totals.

Findings from the preserved study

CAISO weekday daily energy averaged 9.6% above weekends. During the selected September 1?9, 2022 hot spell, daily energy averaged 26.1% above its nearby comparison. These are observed comparisons, not causal effects.

County total energy fell 1.75% in 2020, while residential energy rose 10.4% and non-residential energy fell 5.5%. City of Santa Clara utility January?August 2026 sales were 50.4% above the same 2019 months and 8.3% above 2025. The utility geography differs from the county and 2026 is preliminary.

Adding weather proxies to calendar/trend models improved county residential prediction RMSE by 19.4%, while the tested county-total model had 7.8% larger RMSE. These are prediction-error changes, not shares of electricity caused by weather.

Source-file availability

The hosted page includes only its interactive views, maps, and public chart data. Large raw workbooks, raw API responses, internal workflow records, credentials, and local build caches are retained in the project and are not part of this public website. Where a source is too large for this website, a link leads here instead. Official sources: EIA hourly demand, CEC consumption, Open-Meteo, and EIA utility sales.
